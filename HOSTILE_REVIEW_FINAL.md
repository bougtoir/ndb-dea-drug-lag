# Hostile Review — Hepatitis C DAA manuscript (Hepatology International variant)

Simulated antagonistic referee pass, pre-submission. For each criticism: the reviewer's likely attack, what the manuscript does about it, and whether a further manuscript change was required.

## 1. "Suoh et al. (2026) already did the national DAA utilization analysis — this is redundant."

**Attack:** Suoh et al. quantified ~300,000 treated patients and >1.23 trillion JPY over 2014–2023 with prefecture/demographic detail; a reviewer may see this NDB study as a lower-resolution rerun.

**Response in manuscript:** The Introduction now cites Suoh explicitly alongside Yamashita and frames the study as complementary on a different axis: they quantify who was treated, where, and at what cost; this study quantifies the *replacement dynamics* — speed of displacement of the incumbent interferon standard and the rise/fall of total volume — on a common course-equivalent scale that includes the interferon era (Discussion paragraph with explicit extension-not-duplication sentence; cover letter differentiates explicitly). "First nationwide" claims removed.

## 2. "This is pharmacoepidemiology / health-services research — why Hepatology International?"

**Attack:** Wrong journal fit; should go to a drug-utilization venue.

**Response:** HI's readership is hepatology and Asia-Pacific elimination policy; the manuscript is framed around what the trajectory means for elimination programs in the region (post-switch binding constraint shifting to case-finding/linkage). APASL consensus cited; Japan is a regional bellwether for the 2030 targets. Same argument already survived at JGH-family framing; the Discussion makes the hepatology relevance explicit rather than methodological.

## 3. "Course-equivalents are fabricated — quantities are not patients."

**Attack:** Dividing dispensed units by assumed regimen duration manufactures patient counts.

**Response:** Results label every course number "estimate" and the Methods derive each duration from package inserts/JSH guideline (`data/daa_course_assumptions.csv`); both shorter- and longer-duration anchors are reported as ranges (270,939–305,499 cumulative courses). Sensitivity is quantified, not asserted. The manuscript states repeatedly that courses are not observed patients and validates the order of magnitude against Suoh/Yamashita patient counts in the Discussion.

## 4. "You cannot tell unique patients — switching and retreatment inflate counts."

**Response:** Conceded explicitly: quantities are not patient counts; the estimators use one anchor product per regimen to avoid double-counting co-administered components. Direction of bias is discussed; no incidence claim is made.

## 5. "Suppressed cells ('-' and below-threshold) make your tails meaningless."

**Attack:** Missing low-volume cells bias decline rates.

**Response:** Upper-bound sensitivity series is constructed per-year from each table's listing cap; main-vs-upper declines reported side by side (e.g., DAA peak-to-FY2024 fall 94.0% vs 93.9%; combined −91.4% vs upper-bound analogue). Ribavirin FY2024 reappearance is classified as a listing-scope visibility change (edition 11 lists all products), not resumed use, with the reasoning stated in Methods and Results.

## 6. "The segmented regression is over-interpreted — 11 points, 2-point pre-peak segment."

**Attack:** Slope-change P-values on tiny n are noise-mining.

**Response:** Slope-change and post-peak statistics ARE reported — estimate, 95% CI, and model-based P value — but calibrated: the knot is the *observed* FY2015 peak (not an estimated change point), the 2-observation pre-peak segment is flagged as a descriptive increment rather than a trend, and Methods/Results/Limitations/STROBE all state that slope-change P values are model-based and exploratory, not formal change-point evidence. Emphasis stays on magnitude/speed/dominance and the post-peak trajectory.

## 7. "The decline is just the prevalent pool being exhausted — trivial."

**Attack:** Everyone knows a curative therapy burns through prevalence; no novelty.

**Response:** The manuscript states this interpretation openly and turns it into the finding: the *speed and completeness* of the switch (IFN-free share ~50.9% within one year → ~97%) plus the post-peak decay rate (~23%/yr) are the quantifiable, transferable parameters for planning. The "declining count ≠ program failure" reading is framed as interpretation, not observed fact.

## 8. "Transferability: Japan's subsidy program is unique."

**Response:** Conceded and scoped: the Discussion says the same speed "cannot be inferred" for systems without comparable coverage/subsidy; the transferable content is the measurement framework and the shape of the post-reimbursement trajectory, not the Japan-specific magnitude.

## 9. "FY2024 adds little — near-zero tail."

**Attack:** One extra year of a flat tail.

**Response:** FY2024 is not presented as a headline result; it closes the series through the latest official MHLW edition and, importantly, surfaces the edition-11 listing-scope change (all products listed; ribavirin reappears at 28,508 units) which is itself documented as a data-quality finding. Without FY2024 the package would be a year stale at submission.

## 10. "No mechanism — you can't attribute the speed to reimbursement."

**Response:** Agreed, and now stated that way: "consistent with limited aggregate friction between reimbursement and treatment on an annual scale, although the data do not isolate any specific barrier." Media coverage, subsidy timing, and guidelines are listed as plausible contributors the data cannot separate; constraint language is hedged ("may have shifted").

## Disposition

All ten criticisms are either conceded-with-calibration in the text or answered by the revised framing (substitution dynamics on a course-equivalent scale vs. Suoh's utilization economics). No remaining criticism requires new primary data the NDB source cannot provide. The residual reviewer risk is subjective fit (criticism 2), mitigated by the Asia-Pacific elimination framing in the Discussion and cover letter.
